# Part II Coherence Review

Review date: 2026-05-23

## Argument Arc

Part II forms a coherent progression from influence modes to legitimacy:

1. Chapter 5 defines coercion, persuasion, resonance, and governance by how
   each mode affects agency.
2. Chapter 6 tests influence against resistance and cognitive friction.
3. Chapter 7 explains why some narratives persist after immediate persuasion
   ends.
4. Chapter 8 closes the section by defining the ethical boundary between
   clarification and capture.

The section now has a clear center: influence is not judged only by whether it
works, but by what it does to agency, judgment, procedure, legitimacy, and
truth.

## Issues Found

- The four-mode model remains clear and useful.
- Agency remains the organizing axis across Chapters 5-8.
- Resistance, narrative gravity, and ethics each add a distinct layer rather
  than restating the same claim.
- Chapter 8 closes Part II cleanly and prepares the turn into Part III.
- Disclaimers about non-mechanical influence are present but not excessive.
- No section currently reads like a persuasion-hack manual.

## Edits Made

- No chapter edits were needed during this review.

## Vocabulary Rules

- Use `coercion` for influence that narrows agency.
- Use `persuasion` for influence that addresses judgment.
- Use `resonance` for influence that creates recognition.
- Use `governance` for influence through structured conditions.
- Use `clarification` for influence that preserves judgment, agency,
  procedure, and truth.
- Use `capture` for influence that bypasses judgment, hides constraint, or
  recruits resonance against agency.

## Transition Into Part III

Part III should shift from ethical theory into system failure. Chapter 9 should
show how modern discourse systems optimize emotional velocity, engagement,
identity performance, and scalable pressure in ways that reward capture more
reliably than clarification.

## Readiness for Chapter 9

Part II is ready to hand off into Chapter 9. The next chapter should not reopen
the ethics model. It should apply that model to the collapse dynamics of modern
discourse.
