# Part III Coherence Review

Review date: 2026-05-24

## Argument Arc

Part III now forms a coherent collapse sequence:

1. Chapter 9 defines emotional velocity as speed plus emotional intensity,
   reward, visibility, and social pressure.
2. Chapter 10 shows how high-velocity discourse erodes procedural trust when
   process becomes opaque, inconsistent, or invisible.
3. Chapter 11 explains identity weaponization as a legitimacy failure, not as
   an attack on identity, belonging, memory, or solidarity.
4. Chapter 12 closes the section by showing how AI industrializes existing
   pressure through low-cost variation, personalization, volume, and opacity.

The section moves cleanly from acceleration, to legitimacy failure, to identity
pressure, to AI-scale rhetorical infrastructure.

## Issues Found

- Chapter 9 opens Part III cleanly by shifting from ethical theory into
  incentive pressure.
- Chapter 10 keeps procedural trust distinct from blind institutional trust.
- Chapter 11 treats identity with dignity while criticizing identity as a
  shortcut for legitimacy.
- Chapter 12 frames AI as acceleration infrastructure, not as a moral agent or
  mechanical controller.
- Repeated disclaimers about non-mechanical control are present but still doing
  boundary work; they can be reconsidered in a later full-manuscript edit.
- No chapter currently reads as partisan polemic, AI hype, AI doom, or a tactics
  manual.

## Vocabulary Rules

- Use `emotional velocity` for speed, intensity, repetition, visibility, and
  reward acting together.
- Use `procedural trust` for confidence in visible, constrained, consistent,
  accountable process, not obedience to institutions.
- Use `identity weaponization` for identity being used as a shortcut for
  legitimacy, dismissal, permission, accusation, or belonging.
- Use `industrialized persuasion` for scalable rhetorical variation under weak
  governance.
- Keep `agency`, `legitimacy`, `procedure`, `truth`, and `coherence` visible as
  the ethical anchors.

## Transition Into Part IV

Part IV should shift from collapse diagnosis into governed communication. The
central question is no longer only what content should be allowed, but what
structures make communication answerable to truth, agency, procedure, and
coherence under pressure.

## Readiness for Chapter 13

Chapter 13 is ready to begin Part IV. It should argue that governance matters
more than content because incentives, procedures, visibility, and constraints
shape the discourse environment before any individual message is judged.
