# Part I Coherence Review

Review date: 2026-05-23

## Argument Arc

Part I now forms a coherent conceptual staircase:

1. Communication is not merely information; it is architecture.
2. Persuasion depends on invisible structures around claims.
3. Language creates psychological terrain people move through.
4. Attention is the environment where that terrain becomes socially active.

The chapters build from broad premise to mechanism to felt condition to system
environment. Chapter 4 closes the section by naming attention as the layer that
activates the prior three layers in public discourse.

## Issues Found

- The chapters preserve agency and truth consistently.
- The shared vocabulary is stable: architecture, structure, terrain, attention,
  agency, legitimacy, coherence.
- The non-determinism disclaimers are necessary but slightly repetitive.
- Chapter 4 opens Part II cleanly by naming coercion, persuasion, resonance, and
  governance as distinct influence modes.
- No section currently reads like a persuasion-hack manual.

## Edits Made

- Chapter 1: tightened the agency disclaimer and made the Chapter 2 transition
  explicit.
- Chapter 4: removed a repeated mechanical-control disclaimer while preserving
  the agency boundary.

## Vocabulary Rules

- Use `architecture` for arranged conditions and incentives.
- Use `structure` for the hidden arrangement around claims.
- Use `terrain` for felt psychological conditions created by language.
- Use `attention` for the governed environment where signals become visible,
  repeated, rewarded, and remembered.
- Keep `agency`, `truth`, `legitimacy`, and `coherence` visible as ethical
  anchors.

## Transition Into Part II

Part II is ready to begin with influence systems. Chapter 5 should treat
coercion, persuasion, resonance, and governance as different modes of influence
that alter agency in different ways, rather than as interchangeable tactics.
